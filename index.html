Keep teachers in front of the children at a small school in Karnataka where most have no home to return to.

Shree Shiva Shaikshanika Seva Ashrama is a residential school near Tumakuru. It houses, feeds and teaches children from 1st to 10th standard. About 90% of them have no parents or no safe home. The hardest thing to pay for each month is the teaching. Upahar Giving engages teachers for the children there and pays them directly.

The school

Mr. Lepakshaiah worked as a forest guard near Tumakuru. He had no money and no standing to speak of, but he believed everyone owes something to the people around them. He wanted a place where children without a family could get food, a bed and an education.

Someone gave him a small plot of land and a modest building, free. In 1995 he opened a residential school in it with almost nothing else. It is still running.

Even ₹10,000 a month is hard for the ashrama to find. When a teacher can’t be paid reliably, the school loses them, and the children lose the one stable part of their schooling.

What a gift does

₹10,000, about $115, keeps one teacher teaching for one month. $1,400 keeps one teacher for a year. Our goal is $14,000: a full year of teaching for all ten.

Upahar Giving engages each teacher under a written agreement and pays them directly, every month, into their own account. We take no commission and have no paid staff. Whatever we raise goes to the teaching and meals programme we run at the school and the small cost of running a nonprofit properly.

About us

Upahar Giving Inc. is a small volunteer-run nonprofit in New Jersey. Upahar means “gift” in Hindi. We work alongside the ashrama as its advisory, administrative and fundraising partner, while remaining a separate organisation with its own board.

We do not send money to the school. Indian law sets rules for foreign funds reaching Indian charities, and the ashrama is not yet registered to receive them. Rather than work around that, we run our own programme at the school: we engage the teachers as independent contractors and pay them ourselves, and we buy and serve the children’s meals directly. The school nominates teachers, is consulted on every decision about them, and remains the teachers’ day-to-day point of contact. In time we hope to help the school obtain its own registration.

We have applied for 501(c)(3) recognition. Until it is granted, donations are not yet tax-deductible; once granted, it applies from our formation date in September 2026. Donors in India can also give; write to us for details. Read our partnership brief for the full picture.
